Community Organizing Participatory Action Research

COPAR – “Health in the Hands of the People”

Definition & Values of COPAR

  • COPAR – Community Organizing Participatory Action Research.
  • Definition: A process that empowers people by building capability, enhancing collective capacities, and mobilizing them to solve their own problems.
  • Ensures “Health in the Hands of the People” (LOI 949 theme).
  • Emphasis: People solving own problems, internal capacity first, social movement before technical solutions.

Values of COPAR (LOCOA, 2005):

  • Human Rights – Right to life and self‑determination.
  • Social Justice – Fair access to resources and dignity.
  • Social Responsibility – Solidarity and collective concern.

Core Principles of COPAR

People‑centered
People are both the means and end of development.
Participative
People actively involved in assessment, planning, implementation, evaluation.
Democratic
Majority, especially marginalized, decide and lead.
Developmental
Focused on change and human development.
Process‑oriented
Continuous, evolving, requires monitoring & evaluation.

Phases of COPAR (7 Phases)

1. Pre‑Entry Phase (Preparation)

  • Profile the area, reach out to authorities, establish entry plan, ensure alignment, note risks, touch base with institutions, review selection criteria, yield safe strategy.
  • Mnemonics: SELECT – Severe situation, Existing laws, Lack of access, Exploited, Community oppressed, Target for empowerment.

2. Entry Phase (Integration)

  • Establish presence, notify leaders, take part in community life, respect local norms, yield opportunities for dialogue.
  • Avoid: Padrino approach (false hopes) & Bongga approach (dole-outs).

3. Core Group Formation (CORE‑G)

  • Select members who Communicate well, Objective/neutral, Respected member, Eager to learn, Grassroot background.

4. Community Organizing & Capacity Building (COM‑ORG)

  • Community analyzes issues, organize structures, mobilize for participation, open learning opportunities, recognize potentials, grow in capacity.

5. Community Action Phase (ACT ION)

  • Apply solutions, coordinate with stakeholders, tap resources, initiate participation, organize & train CHWs, nurture ownership.
  • ARAS: Action‑Reflection‑Action Session – do, think, improve.

6. Sustenance & Strengthening Phase (S-T-R-O-N-G)

  • Sustain initiatives, transfer leadership, reinforce local capabilities, organize linkages, nurture independence, generate long‑term health plan.

7. Turn Over / Termination

  • Endorsement, turnover of leadership to community.

Four Levels of Care in COPAR/CHN

Individual – Entry point
Family – Focus of care / Focal point
Population Group – Point of specific care
Community – Point of entire care

COPAR in the PHN Role:

  • Facilitator and enabler, not director.
  • Raises awareness, mobilizes community for collective action.
  • Trains BHWs and community leaders.
  • Advocates for health policies and resource allocation.

PHN Role in COPAR Process

  • Pre‑entry: Conducts community profile, coordinates with LGU leaders.
  • Entry: Establishes rapport, integrates with community, avoids padrino/bongga approaches.
  • Core group formation: Identifies potential leaders with CORE‑G traits.
  • Organizing & capacity building: Facilitates training, helps community analyze problems.
  • Action phase: Guides ARAS sessions, mobilizes resources, trains CHWs.
  • Sustenance & strengthening: Supports leadership transfer, builds linkages.
  • Termination: Ensures smooth turnover and community ownership.

Key principle: The PHN is a catalyst – the community must eventually lead without dependency.
